Bring Frida back to the corner.

For six years a portrait of Frida Kahlo greeted everyone who stopped at Cavalcade and Irvington. We are inviting neighbors and organizations to help paint her again.

The story so far

A small box with a big place in the neighborhood.

Houston's Mini Murals program turns ordinary traffic signal cabinets into neighborhood art. In 2016, artist Anat Ronen painted Frida Kahlo on the cabinet at Cavalcade and Irvington. People walked by it, drove past it, and waited at the light beside it for years.

In September 2022 a car crash destroyed the cabinet. The plain gray box that replaced it has been there ever since. Neighbors have been asking how to bring her back, and more people are joining in.

    For sponsors

    Funding commitment letter

    The city's Mini Murals program starts with a sponsor. This letter tells the Mayor's Office of the Arts and the program who is funding the mural. Put it on your letterhead, fill in the highlighted parts, sign it, and send it to us. We will submit it with the city's mural inquiry form. The sponsor also completes the program's Sponsorship Application at minimurals.org, which leads to a Sponsorship Agreement and a written quote.

    What the sponsor application asks

    The Sponsorship Application takes a few minutes. It asks for the organization or business name; a primary contact with name, email and phone; website and social links (optional); a specific neighborhood or intersection (optional); your vision for the mural (optional); and how many murals you would like to sponsor. Suggested answers for this project:

    Intersection: Cavalcade St at Irvington Blvd, Houston 77009 (Near Northside, Council District H). The cabinet held Anat Ronen's 2016 Frida Kahlo mural until a 2022 crash.

    Vision: Restore the Frida Kahlo portrait that neighbors loved, painted by the original artist, Anat Ronen.

    Questions

    What people ask.

    Can we request the mural now?

    Not yet. The city takes a mural request only after a sponsor or funding commitment is in place, so finding a sponsor is the first step. Once we have one, we submit the city's mural inquiry form, and the city tells us whether the cabinet is available.

    Who is organizing this?

    A Near Northside neighbor, with the help of the artist and the city's Mini Murals program. We are looking for organizations to join and share the work.

    Is Frida Kahlo allowed under the city's rules?

    Yes. The city's program does not allow living individuals, logos or ads, and Frida Kahlo is deceased. The original mural was part of the same program.

    What does the city require?

    A sponsor or funding commitment, an inquiry form, an agreement with liability insurance, artwork approval, and notice to the Houston Police Department before painting. The city estimates 6 to 8 weeks for the administrative steps.

    Where will the money go?

    The Mini Murals sponsorship fee is all-inclusive. It includes the artist's stipend and materials, plus project management, cabinet preparation and coating, photography, community engagement and press. We expect roughly $3,000 to $5,000 and will post the final figure once the program issues a written quote. Sponsors are credited in the program's project materials and on minimurals.org.

    What if the cabinet is not available?

    We have a plan B. The artist has offered to paint a wall mural nearby with the owner's permission, and other cabinets in the neighborhood are possible.

    Bring Frida Back is a neighborhood effort and is not an official City of Houston website. Mini Murals is a program of the City of Houston.
